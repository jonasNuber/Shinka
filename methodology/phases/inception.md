# Inception Phase

## Purpose

The purpose of the Inception phase is to **consciously decide whether a version should exist**, and if so, **why**.

Inception is not about detailed planning or design.
It is about intent, scope, and direction.

A good Inception answers:
- Why are we doing this?
- What problem is being addressed?
- What is explicitly *out of scope*?
- Is this version worth the effort right now?

---

## Position in the Lifecycle

Inception is the **entry point for every new version** of a project.

It occurs:
- At the start of a new project
- At the start of a new version of an existing project
- When a paused project is resumed

Inception is considered complete when there is a **clear and shared understanding of intent** and a decision to proceed.

---

## Core Outcomes

By the end of Inception, the following should be true:

- The **purpose** of the version is clear
- The **scope boundaries** are defined
- Key **assumptions and risks** are visible
- There is a conscious **go / no-go decision**

Inception does *not* require certainty — only clarity.

---

## Minimum Viable Inception (MVI)

Shinka defines a **Minimum Viable Inception** to prevent overthinking and premature design.

At minimum, the following must exist:

### 1. Version Intent

A short statement describing:
- What this version is trying to achieve
- Why now
- For whom

This can be as small as a few sentences.

---

### 2. Scope Definition

Explicitly define:
- What is included
- What is intentionally excluded

Out-of-scope items are as important as in-scope items.

---

### 3. Success Criteria

Define what “done” means for this version.

Examples:
- A user can complete a specific workflow
- A technical capability is proven
- A hypothesis is validated

Success criteria should be observable.

---

### 4. Go / No-Go Decision

A conscious decision to:
- Proceed to Elaboration
- Pause the idea
- Discard the version entirely

This decision should be explicit, even for solo projects.

---

## Typical Activities

Depending on project size and uncertainty, Inception may include:

- Writing a project or version vision
- Identifying primary users or stakeholders
- High-level problem exploration
- Early feasibility checks
- Rough prioritization of goals

None of these activities are mandatory beyond what is needed to achieve the core outcomes.

---

## Artifacts (Optional but Recommended)

Inception may produce or update:

- Project or version vision document
- Initial user or problem statements
- Early backlog items or epics
- Initial assumptions list
- Initial risk notes

All artifacts should be lightweight and easy to revise or discard.

---

## What Inception Is *Not*

Inception explicitly avoids:

- Detailed architecture design
- Technology selection without justification
- Full backlog decomposition
- Implementation planning

Those belong in later phases.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Skipping Inception entirely**  
  Leads to unclear goals and reactive development.

- **Overloading Inception**  
  Turning it into detailed planning or design delays feedback unnecessarily.

- **Implicit decisions**  
  Proceeding without a conscious go/no-go decision causes long-term drift.

---

## Solo vs Team Considerations

### Solo Development

- Inception is often informal but should still be explicit.
- Writing things down reduces cognitive load and future confusion.
- The primary value is clarity and intentionality.

### Small Teams

- Inception aligns understanding early.
- Misalignment discovered later is significantly more costly.
- Even a short shared document can prevent major misunderstandings.

---

## Transition to Elaboration

Inception transitions to Elaboration when:

- The version intent is clear
- Scope boundaries are defined
- A decision to proceed has been made

Elaboration builds on Inception by answering:
- What exactly will be built?
- How should it be structured?
- What risks must be addressed first?

---

## Inception as a Repeating Skill

In Shinka, Inception is not a one-time ceremony.
It is a **repeating skill** applied at the start of every version.

Good Inception enables:
- Faster Elaboration
- Focused Construction
- Meaningful releases
- Healthier long-term projects
